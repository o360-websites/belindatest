Joint & Musculoskeletal Health

Types of Arthritis

Learn about common types of arthritis, symptoms, causes, diagnosis, and treatment options to reduce pain and protect joint function.

Arthritis is a general term for conditions that affect the joints and nearby tissues. It can cause pain, stiffness, swelling, warmth, and trouble with movement. There are many types of arthritis, and the right treatment depends on the specific cause. Arthritis can affect people of any age. Early evaluation is important when symptoms are persistent, worsening, or interfering with daily activities.

Common Arthritis Symptoms

Symptoms vary by type of arthritis and by the joints involved. Common symptoms include:

  • Joint pain or tenderness
  • Stiffness, especially after resting or in the morning
  • Swelling, warmth, or redness around a joint
  • Reduced movement or flexibility
  • Weakness or difficulty with daily activities
  • Fatigue or feeling unwell, especially with inflammatory arthritis

Common Types of Arthritis

Osteoarthritis

Osteoarthritis is the most common type of arthritis. It involves changes in the whole joint, including cartilage, bone, and nearby tissues. Cartilage is the smooth tissue that helps bones move easily at a joint. Osteoarthritis often affects the knees, hips, hands, spine, and feet. Pain may worsen with activity and improve with rest. Stiffness is usually brief after waking or sitting for a long time. Previous injury, repeated joint stress, aging, and excess body weight can increase risk.

Rheumatoid Arthritis

Rheumatoid arthritis is an autoimmune disease. This means the immune system mistakenly attacks healthy joint tissue, causing inflammation. It often affects the same joints on both sides of the body, such as both hands, wrists, or feet. Symptoms can include persistent joint swelling, pain, prolonged morning stiffness, fatigue, and difficulty with daily tasks. Early treatment can help control inflammation and protect joints.

Psoriatic Arthritis

Psoriatic arthritis is an inflammatory arthritis that can occur in people with psoriasis, a skin condition that may cause scaly patches. Some people develop joint symptoms before they notice skin changes. Symptoms may include painful or swollen joints, swollen fingers or toes, heel pain, back stiffness, fatigue, and nail changes such as pitting or separation from the nail bed.

Gout

Gout is a type of inflammatory arthritis caused by a buildup of uric acid crystals in and around a joint. It often causes sudden, severe pain with swelling, warmth, and redness. Gout commonly affects the big toe but can also affect the ankle, knee, wrist, elbow, or other joints. Symptoms often begin quickly and may occur at night. Treatment can address current flares and help prevent future attacks.

Ankylosing Spondylitis

Ankylosing spondylitis is an inflammatory arthritis that mainly affects the spine and the joints where the spine connects to the pelvis. It can cause ongoing low back or buttock pain and stiffness that is often worse in the morning or after rest and may improve with movement. Some people may also have pain or swelling in other joints, heel pain, fatigue, or eye inflammation. Early evaluation and treatment can help manage symptoms and maintain mobility.

Juvenile Arthritis

Juvenile arthritis is arthritis that begins during childhood. There are several forms, and symptoms may include joint pain, swelling, stiffness, limping, fatigue, or fever. Children with ongoing joint symptoms should be evaluated so they can receive appropriate treatment and support for healthy growth and activity.

Lupus Arthritis

Lupus is an autoimmune disease that can affect joints, skin, kidneys, blood, and other parts of the body. Lupus arthritis may cause joint pain, swelling, stiffness, and fatigue. Some people with lupus also have a rash, sensitivity to sunlight, mouth sores, fever, or other symptoms. Evaluation is important because lupus can affect more than the joints.

Reactive Arthritis

Reactive arthritis is joint inflammation that may develop after an infection, often in the digestive tract or urinary tract. It can cause joint pain and swelling, usually in the knees, ankles, or feet. Some people may also have eye redness or pain, urinary symptoms, or skin changes. Symptoms may improve over time, but medical evaluation is important to guide care and rule out other causes.

How Is Arthritis Diagnosed?

An arthritis evaluation may include:

  • Discussion of symptoms, health history, and family history
  • Physical examination of the joints, skin, nails, spine, and movement
  • Blood tests when inflammation, autoimmune disease, or gout is suspected
  • X-rays, ultrasound, MRI, or other imaging when needed
  • Joint fluid testing in selected cases, especially for sudden joint swelling or possible gout or infection

There is no single test that identifies every type of arthritis. The pattern of symptoms, examination, and test results helps determine the cause.

Arthritis Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the type of arthritis, affected joints, symptom severity, and overall health. A personalized plan may include:

  • Regular gentle movement, stretching, and strengthening
  • Physical therapy or occupational therapy
  • Activity changes and joint-protection strategies
  • Heat for stiffness or cold packs for pain and swelling
  • Weight management when appropriate
  • Braces, splints, canes, or other supportive devices
  • Medicines to manage pain or inflammation
  • Medicines that control immune-system inflammation for autoimmune arthritis
  • Medicines that lower uric acid for gout when appropriate
  • Joint injections or surgery for selected patients

Living Well With Arthritis

  • Stay active with low-impact activities, such as walking, swimming, cycling, or chair exercises.
  • Start slowly and increase activity at a comfortable pace.
  • Balance activity with rest and break larger tasks into smaller steps.
  • Use supportive footwear and assistive devices when needed.
  • Prioritize sleep, stress management, and a balanced eating pattern.
  • Avoid smoking, which can worsen inflammatory arthritis and affect overall health.
  • Follow your treatment plan and report new symptoms or medication concerns.

When to Seek Urgent Medical Care

Seek urgent medical attention if you have:

  • A joint that is suddenly very painful, hot, red, or severely swollen
  • Fever, chills, or feeling very unwell along with joint pain or swelling
  • Inability to bear weight after an injury
  • Sudden weakness, numbness, or loss of joint function
  • A painful red eye, vision changes, or light sensitivity along with back or joint symptoms

These symptoms may be caused by joint infection, injury, or another condition that needs prompt evaluation.
