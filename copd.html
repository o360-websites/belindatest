Respiratory Health

COPD

Learn about COPD symptoms, causes, diagnosis, treatment, pulmonary rehabilitation, flare-up signs, and when to seek emergency care.

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, or COPD, is a long-term lung condition that makes it harder to move air in and out of the lungs. COPD commonly includes emphysema and chronic bronchitis. COPD can cause ongoing breathing symptoms, but treatment and healthy habits can help reduce symptoms, prevent flare-ups, and support an active, fulfilling life.

Common Symptoms of COPD

Symptoms often develop gradually and may worsen over time. Common symptoms include:

  • Shortness of breath, especially with activity
  • Ongoing cough
  • Mucus or phlegm production
  • Wheezing or chest tightness
  • Feeling tired or having less energy
  • Frequent respiratory infections
  • Difficulty doing usual activities, such as walking, climbing stairs, or household tasks

Some people may have periods when symptoms suddenly become worse. This is called a COPD flare-up or exacerbation.

What Causes COPD?

Smoking is the most common cause of COPD. However, COPD can also develop in people who have had long-term exposure to:

  • Secondhand smoke
  • Workplace dust, fumes, or chemicals
  • Air pollution
  • Smoke from burning wood, charcoal, or other fuels
  • Certain inherited conditions that affect the lungs

Not everyone with COPD has a history of smoking. Identifying and reducing exposure to lung irritants is an important part of treatment.

How Is COPD Diagnosed?

COPD is diagnosed using a combination of symptoms, medical history, physical examination, and breathing tests. The main test is called spirometry. During spirometry, you breathe into a machine that measures how much air you can move and how quickly you can breathe it out. This test helps confirm COPD and assess lung function.

Your evaluation may also include:

  • Review of your symptoms and exposure history
  • Chest X-ray or CT scan when needed
  • Oxygen-level testing
  • Blood tests in selected situations
  • Evaluation for other conditions that can affect breathing

COPD Treatment Options

There is no cure for COPD, but treatment can help you breathe easier, stay active, and lower the chance of flare-ups. Your treatment plan is based on your symptoms, lung function, past flare-ups, and overall health. Treatment may include:

  • Inhalers that relax and open the airways
  • Other medicines to reduce symptoms or help prevent flare-ups
  • A personalized plan for managing worsening symptoms
  • Pulmonary rehabilitation
  • Oxygen therapy for selected patients with low oxygen levels
  • Vaccinations to help reduce the risk of serious respiratory infections
  • Treatment for related health conditions, such as heart disease, anxiety, sleep problems, or depression

Pulmonary Rehabilitation

Pulmonary rehabilitation is a structured program for people with chronic lung disease. It may include exercise training, breathing techniques, education, nutrition guidance, and emotional support. This program can help improve strength, endurance, confidence, and quality of life.

Everyday Tips for Living With COPD

  • Take your medicines exactly as prescribed.
  • Review your inhaler technique regularly to make sure the medicine is reaching your lungs.
  • Avoid smoking and exposure to secondhand smoke.
  • Stay active with a safe exercise plan that fits your abilities.
  • Use breathing techniques, such as pursed-lip breathing, when you feel short of breath.
  • Eat balanced meals and stay hydrated.
  • Get recommended vaccines.
  • Avoid exposure to dust, fumes, strong scents, smoke, and poor air quality when possible.
  • Keep follow-up appointments and discuss any changes in symptoms.

Recognizing a COPD Flare-Up

A flare-up is a sudden worsening of COPD symptoms that may require additional treatment. Contact our office promptly if you notice:

  • More shortness of breath than usual
  • Increased coughing or wheezing
  • More mucus, or mucus that is thicker or changes color
  • New chest tightness
  • Fever or symptoms of a respiratory infection
  • Needing to use your rescue inhaler more often than usual
  • Trouble sleeping or doing routine activities because of breathing symptoms

When to Seek Emergency Care

Call 911 or seek emergency care for:

  • Severe shortness of breath or trouble speaking in full sentences
  • Blue, gray, or pale lips, face, or fingertips
  • New confusion, extreme sleepiness, or fainting
  • Severe chest pain or pressure
  • Symptoms that do not improve with your rescue medicine as directed

Living Well With COPD

A COPD diagnosis can feel overwhelming, but you do not have to manage it alone. Following your treatment plan, avoiding lung irritants, staying active, and recognizing flare-up symptoms early can help you protect your lung health and maintain independence.
