Memory & Brain Health

Alzheimer’s Disease

Learn about Alzheimer’s disease signs, diagnosis, treatment, daily living tips, safety planning, and caregiver support from Noor Health Clinic.

Alzheimer’s disease is the most common cause of dementia. Dementia is a general term for problems with memory, thinking, and daily functioning that become severe enough to affect everyday life. Alzheimer’s disease is not a normal part of aging. It is a progressive brain condition, meaning symptoms gradually worsen over time. Each person’s experience is different.

Common Signs and Symptoms

Early symptoms may include:

  • Forgetting recently learned information or important dates
  • Repeating questions or stories
  • Misplacing items and being unable to retrace steps
  • Difficulty finding words or following conversations
  • Trouble managing finances, medications, appointments, or familiar tasks
  • Becoming confused about time, place, or directions
  • Changes in judgment, mood, personality, or social interest

Memory changes can have many causes. A complete evaluation can help identify the cause and guide care.

How Alzheimer’s Disease Is Diagnosed

There is no single test for Alzheimer’s disease. Evaluation may include:

  • A detailed health and memory history
  • Questions about daily functioning and changes noticed by family or friends
  • Physical and neurologic examination
  • Memory and thinking tests
  • Blood tests to look for other possible causes of symptoms
  • Brain imaging, such as MRI or CT, when appropriate
  • Additional specialized testing in selected situations

Treatment and Care

Although there is currently no cure, treatment can help support quality of life and manage symptoms. A care plan may include:

  • Medicines for memory or thinking symptoms when appropriate
  • Treatment for depression, anxiety, sleep problems, or behavioral symptoms when present
  • Regular physical activity and social engagement
  • Management of hearing, vision, blood pressure, diabetes, and other health conditions
  • Simple routines and a calm, familiar environment
  • Support for caregivers and family members

Everyday Tips for Living With Memory Changes

Helpful strategies may include:

  • Keep a regular daily schedule.
  • Use calendars, labels, reminders, and written instructions.
  • Keep commonly used items in the same place.
  • Focus on one task at a time.
  • Reduce background noise and clutter.
  • Encourage safe physical activity, healthy meals, hydration, and regular sleep.
  • Include the person in decisions as much as possible.

Safety Planning

As memory and judgment change, safety needs may change too. Families may wish to plan ahead for:

  • Medication organization and supervision
  • Driving safety
  • Fall prevention and home safety
  • Preventing wandering or getting lost
  • Financial and legal planning
  • Advance care planning and identifying a healthcare decision-maker

Support for Caregivers

Caring for someone with Alzheimer’s disease can be meaningful and challenging. Caregivers may benefit from help with transportation, meals, respite care, support groups, and education. Taking breaks and caring for your own health are important parts of caregiving.

When to Seek Urgent Help

Seek urgent medical evaluation for sudden confusion or a rapid change in behavior, alertness, speech, strength, balance, breathing, or level of consciousness. Sudden changes may be caused by a medical problem other than Alzheimer’s disease and should be assessed promptly.
