Heart & Cardiovascular Health

Heart Disease

Learn how Noor Health Clinic helps prevent and manage heart disease with personalized care for high blood pressure, high cholesterol, heart failure, and remote patient monitoring.

At Noor Health Clinic, we provide personalized primary care to help prevent and manage heart disease. Our team works with patients who have high blood pressure (hypertension), high cholesterol, heart failure, or other risk factors for heart disease and stroke. Heart disease may develop over time and may not cause noticeable symptoms in its early stages. Regular checkups, blood pressure monitoring, cholesterol testing, healthy lifestyle habits, and medications when appropriate can help protect your heart and overall health.

Understanding Heart Disease

Heart disease is a broad term for conditions that affect the heart and blood vessels. Common risk factors include high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, limited physical activity, excess weight, family history, and older age.

Managing these risks can lower the chance of heart attack, stroke, kidney problems, and worsening heart function.

High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)

High blood pressure means blood is pushing too strongly against the walls of your arteries. It often causes no symptoms, which is why it is sometimes called a “silent” condition. Over time, untreated high blood pressure can damage the heart, brain, kidneys, and blood vessels.

How Noor Health Clinic Can Help

Our primary care team can help you:

  • Check and track your blood pressure over time.
  • Review home blood pressure readings.
  • Identify factors that may raise blood pressure, including certain medicines, stress, sleep problems, or kidney disease.
  • Create a heart-healthy nutrition and activity plan.
  • Prescribe and adjust blood pressure medicines when appropriate.
  • Coordinate specialty care when needed.
  • Determine whether remote patient monitoring services may be appropriate for you.

High Cholesterol

Cholesterol is a waxy substance in the blood. While the body needs some cholesterol, too much LDL cholesterol, often called “bad” cholesterol, can build up in blood vessels. This buildup can narrow arteries and increase the risk of heart attack and stroke.

High cholesterol usually does not cause symptoms. A simple blood test, called a lipid panel, is the best way to check your cholesterol levels.

How Noor Health Clinic Can Help

Your primary care team can:

  • Order cholesterol screening and follow-up testing.
  • Review your personal and family history of heart disease.
  • Discuss your overall risk for heart attack and stroke.
  • Provide guidance on heart-healthy eating, physical activity, and tobacco cessation.
  • Discuss cholesterol-lowering medicines when appropriate.
  • Monitor your progress and help you make sustainable changes.

Heart Failure

Heart failure does not mean the heart has stopped. It means the heart is not pumping blood as well as it should. This may cause fluid to build up in the body and can make it harder to breathe or complete daily activities.

Heart failure may be related to high blood pressure, coronary artery disease, prior heart attack, heart valve problems, diabetes, or other conditions.

Common Symptoms of Heart Failure

  • Shortness of breath with activity or while lying down
  • Swelling in the feet, ankles, legs, or abdomen
  • Rapid weight gain from fluid buildup
  • Tiredness or weakness
  • Coughing or wheezing, especially at night
  • Decreased ability to walk, climb stairs, or perform usual activities
  • Fast or irregular heartbeat

How Noor Health Clinic Can Help

For patients with heart failure, Noor Health Clinic provides ongoing primary care support, including:

  • Monitoring symptoms, weight changes, blood pressure, and medication side effects.
  • Reviewing medications and helping patients understand how and when to take them.
  • Supporting low-sodium eating and other individualized lifestyle changes.
  • Helping identify new or worsening symptoms early.
  • Coordinating care with cardiology and other specialists.
  • Supporting follow-up after emergency department visits or hospital stays.
  • Discussing remote patient monitoring services for eligible patients.

Remote Patient Monitoring Services

Remote patient monitoring (RPM) is a service that allows eligible patients to use connected health devices at home. The devices securely send health readings to the Noor Health Clinic care team between regular office visits.

RPM can be helpful for patients who need closer follow-up for high blood pressure, heart failure, or other chronic health conditions. It gives patients a convenient way to share home readings and helps the care team see health trends over time.

What Is Included in Remote Patient Monitoring Services

For eligible patients, remote patient monitoring services may include:

  • A connected home device, such as a blood pressure cuff or digital scale, based on your health needs.
  • Help setting up and learning how to use the device.
  • A personalized plan for when and how often to check your readings.
  • Secure transmission of home readings to the care team.
  • Ongoing review of readings and trends by the care team.
  • Follow-up outreach when readings are outside your personalized range or when additional information is needed.
  • Education and support for medications, nutrition, activity, and other heart-healthy habits.
  • Coordination with your primary care provider and specialists when appropriate.
  • Regular follow-up visits to review progress and update your care plan.

For patients with high blood pressure, RPM may focus on home blood pressure and heart rate readings. For patients with heart failure, RPM may also include tracking daily weight and symptoms to help identify possible fluid buildup early.

Remote patient monitoring is not emergency care and does not replace regular office visits or specialty care. If you have chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting, stroke symptoms, or another medical emergency, call 911 immediately.

Everyday Steps for Better Heart Health

Small changes can have an important effect on heart health.

  • Take medications exactly as prescribed.
  • Check blood pressure and weight as directed.
  • Choose more vegetables, fruits, whole grains, beans, and lean proteins.
  • Limit sodium, highly processed foods, and sugary drinks.
  • Stay active in ways that are safe for you.
  • Avoid smoking and secondhand smoke.
  • Limit alcohol if advised by your care team.
  • Keep regular appointments and complete recommended blood tests.
  • Bring your medication list and home readings to your visits.

When to Contact Noor Health Clinic

Please contact Noor Health Clinic for:

  • Blood pressure readings that are repeatedly outside your personal goal range
  • New medication concerns or side effects
  • New or worsening swelling
  • Increased shortness of breath
  • Rapid weight gain or other possible signs of fluid buildup
  • New fatigue or difficulty completing usual activities
  • Questions about cholesterol testing, blood pressure care, heart failure management, or remote patient monitoring services

When to Seek Emergency Care

Call 911 or seek emergency care right away for:

  • Chest pain, pressure, squeezing, or discomfort
  • Severe shortness of breath
  • Fainting, new confusion, or sudden severe weakness
  • Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body
  • Trouble speaking, sudden vision changes, or a severe headache
  • Blue, gray, or pale lips or fingertips
